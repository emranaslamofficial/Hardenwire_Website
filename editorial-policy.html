    Editorial Policy

    The standards every Hardenwire article is held to, from first draft to its latest update.

    Last updated Nov 1, 2026

    Independence

    Hardenwire does not accept payment for coverage, sponsored articles, or affiliate commissions. Companies cannot pay to be included, reviewed favourably, or left out. If that ever changes, for example if we introduce clearly labelled sponsorship of the newsletter, we will update this policy first and label any sponsored material prominently.

    Writers must disclose any financial or personal relationship with a company or product they write about. Where a conflict exists, a different writer takes the assignment.

    Research and sourcing

    • We prefer primary sources: standards bodies (such as NIST and the FIDO Alliance), government agencies (CISA, FTC, NCSC, FBI IC3), official vendor documentation, and peer-reviewed or well-documented security research.
    • Statistics are attributed to their original source, with the year. If a figure comes from secondary reporting we say so, and we avoid numbers we can't trace.
    • Major factual claims are linked in a Sources section at the end of each article.
    • We don't cite competitors' blogs as authorities, and we're cautious with vendor-funded research, which we label as such.

    Testing

    Step-by-step guides are followed on real, current devices and software before publication. Where steps vary by manufacturer or version, we say so rather than pretending there's one universal path.

    Review

    Every article is edited by a second member of the editorial team, who checks the accuracy, clarity and tone of the advice. We ask: is this proportionate? Does it tell readers what not to worry about as well as what to do?

    Keeping guides current

    Security and privacy settings change with every operating system, browser and app release. We:

    • Review settings walkthroughs after major platform releases, and every article at least once a year.
    • Show both a Published and an Updated date. We change the updated date only when the content has materially changed, not for cosmetic edits.
    • Correct errors under our corrections policy.

    Responsible coverage

    We write about attacks in order to help people defend against them. We don't publish working exploit code, step-by-step instructions for compromising systems or accounts, or techniques whose main use is harming others. When we write about scams, we describe the pattern without reproducing material that would help someone run the scam.

    Use of AI tools

    Editors may use software tools, including AI-assisted tools, for tasks such as transcription, spelling and grammar checks, or summarising long technical documents during research. Every article is written, fact-checked and approved by a named human editor who is accountable for its accuracy. We don't publish unreviewed machine-generated content.

    Contact

    Questions about this policy can be sent through our contact page.
