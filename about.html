    About Hardenwire

    We explain how attacks and tracking actually work, tell you which defences matter, and give you steps you can follow without a computer science degree.

    Why we exist

    Most security advice online falls into one of two groups. Government guidance is trustworthy but often brief and slow to update. Vendor blogs are detailed but written to sell the vendor's product. Readers are left between the two, unsure whether they really need antivirus on a phone, whether a VPN will protect them, or what to do in the hour after clicking a bad link.

    Hardenwire fills that gap. We're an independent publication that explains cybersecurity and privacy for people who use technology, rather than people who build it, and we treat proportion as part of good advice: telling you what you don't need to worry about matters as much as what you do.

    What we cover

    Our coverage is organised into three hubs:

    • Cybersecurity: protecting accounts, devices and home networks, from passwords and passkeys to malware and routers.
    • Privacy: tracking, data brokers, VPNs and identity protection.
    • Online safety: phishing and scams, fraud and impersonation.

    How we're different

    No affiliate links. We don't earn commission when you buy a VPN, password manager or antivirus product. When we name a product, it's because it illustrates a point or is genuinely the common choice.

    Defensive only. We explain how attacks work so that you can recognise and stop them. We don't publish instructions for attacking systems or people.

    Tested and dated. Step-by-step guides are followed on real devices before publication, and each one shows when it was last reviewed. Our editorial policy sets out how we research, source and update our work, and our corrections policy explains what happens when we get something wrong.

    Who we are

    Hardenwire is written and edited by a small team of editors and reporters. You can read about each of them on our authors page. Every article has a named author who is accountable for it.

    Get in touch

    We welcome scam tips, corrections and suggestions. Write to us through the contact page, or subscribe to the Briefing, our weekly newsletter.
